# CTCI III–V: Special Situations, Before the Interview, Behavioral Questions

## Chapter III: Special Situations (mostly not for me)
- Experienced hires get more system design and higher expectations. Testers, PMs and managers each get their own mix of questions.
- **Startups:** often hire through personal connections. They may care more about your experience and specific languages, and less about classic DS&A.
- **For interviewers (useful for mocks):**
  - Don't ask questions straight from this book, since candidates may have seen them
  - Medium to hard questions work best. Avoid "scary" ones that freeze people up.
  - Give positive reinforcement and push on vague behavioural answers
  - Coach the candidate a little if they're stuck

## Chapter IV: Before the Interview

### Getting the right experience
- Students: internships, classes with big projects, side projects
- Code outside school and work. It shows on the resume and in interviews.

### Resume
- One page unless you have 10+ years of experience
- Bullet format: **accomplished X by implementing Y, which led to Z**. Use numbers where you can.
- 2 to 4 projects: what you built, the tech, and whether it was solo or team
- Languages: only list ones you're comfortable being tested on, and add a qualifier like "proficient" or "familiar"

### Prep map
- Long-term: projects, portfolio, network
- 3 to 12 months before: CTCI intros, Big O, implement DS&A from scratch, mock group, list of mistakes
- Last month: mocks, rehearse stories, keep doing questions
- Day of: talk out loud, struggling is normal, be confident but not cocky
- After: follow up with the recruiter after a week. If there's no offer, ask when you can reapply.

## Chapter V: Behavioral Questions

### Prep grid
- Rows: projects and jobs. Columns: challenges, mistakes or failures, enjoyed, leadership, conflicts, what you'd do differently.
- Have stories ready for each cell. *(Filling mine after better projects and Nutanix.)*

### Know your technical projects
- Pick 2 to 3 projects you can go deep on: hard parts, your role, and the technical decisions you made

### Weaknesses
- Give a real one, not a fake like "I work too hard". Say what you're doing about it.

### Questions to ask the interviewer
- **Genuine:** what you actually want to know (the team, the day-to-day)
- **Insightful:** questions that show you've looked into their tech or product
- **Passion:** questions that show interest in learning or a specific area

### How to answer
- Be specific, not arrogant. Let the facts show you're good.
- Keep the detail light and let them ask for more
- Say "I", not "we". They're assessing you.
- **Nugget first:** open with a one-line summary of the story
- **S.A.R.:** Situation, Action, Result. Action matters most, so break it into steps.
- Think about what the story shows about you: initiative, leadership, empathy, teamwork, humility

### "Tell me about yourself"
1. Current role, as a one-line headline
2. University
3. Experience since then
4. What you do now, in more detail
5. Outside work, only if it's relevant (side projects, clubs)
6. Wrap up: what you're looking for and why this company

Show your successes along the way without bragging.
